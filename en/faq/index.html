Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to your questions about the DSA SMART START method.

General approach for DSA learners

How is the DSA SMART START programme designed to support students with dyslexia in learning English?

DSA SMART START is designed around an inclusive approach that uses multisensory techniques, visual tools and adaptive strategies to make learning English easier. Each module is accessible and engaging, respecting the learning needs of students with dyslexia.

What are the main features that make DSA SMART START suitable for students with dyslexia?

Key features include lively graphics, interactive activities, mind maps, colour coding to improve visual organisation and audio support to aid understanding. These elements encourage engaging learning that reduces reading and memorisation difficulties.

How does the programme differ from traditional teaching methods to meet the needs of students with dyslexia?

DSA SMART START integrates teaching techniques that use shapes, colours, visual diagrams and audio support. Unlike traditional methods, the programme emphasises visual and practical learning to strengthen understanding and memorisation.

Which visual and learning techniques does DSA SMART START use to support memorisation and understanding?

Mind maps, colour-coded diagrams and illustrations present information visually. These techniques help students see connections between ideas and organise their thinking, improving memorisation and understanding.

How are shapes and colours used to improve learning for students with dyslexia?

Shapes and colours highlight important parts of lessons, distinguish word categories and represent grammatical concepts. This technique is called SHAPE & COLOR CODING BRAIN PUZZLE and reduces visual complexity, helping students focus on key information.

How are mind maps integrated into the programme to organise information and support learning?

Mind maps summarise lessons and visually represent connections between concepts. They help students with dyslexia structure information in a more logical and memorable way.

How does the programme use visual impact to support understanding and memorisation?

The programme combines colourful images, charts and visual diagrams that intuitively represent words and sentences. This helps students connect meanings with images, making memorisation more natural.

Visual and multisensory approach

What role do images and visual materials play in teaching English with DSA SMART START?

Images and visual materials are central to building strong mental associations and understanding content. They turn words into visual concepts that are easier for students with dyslexia to process and remember.

How does the programme use illustrations to reinforce the meaning of words and sentences?

Illustrations accompany new vocabulary and key sentences in every lesson. This helps students connect images with words, strengthening understanding and supporting visual learning.

How does the visual approach accelerate memorisation and create a lasting memory?

The visual approach creates strong connections between images and language concepts, accelerating memorisation. These connections help create a lasting impression in long-term memory, making learning more effective.

Adaptations for understanding and practising the language

How does the programme support language comprehension through adaptive learning techniques for students with dyslexia?

The programme includes simplified reading, video support for listening comprehension, and diagrams and maps to summarise content. This enables students to learn at their own pace and according to their needs.

Which specific tools are used to improve listening and written comprehension for students with dyslexia?

DSA SMART START uses guided video explanations to improve listening comprehension. For writing, visual guides, sentence models and interactive exercises support practice.

How does DSA SMART START make speaking and writing practice more accessible for students with dyslexia?

The programme includes practical activities that encourage conversation and writing, using visual prompts to aid understanding. Exercises are repetitive but varied, maintaining interest and promoting learning.

Techniques for lasting learning

Which strategies does DSA SMART START use to make learning lasting and well established?

The programme uses spaced repetition and interactive practice to reinforce learnt concepts. Mind maps and image-based activities consolidate learning and help it last.

How are visual elements and multisensory techniques used to build stable mental connections?

Visual elements and multisensory techniques, such as linking words with sounds, images and colours, create multiple mental connections that support memorisation and recall.

Benefits and expected outcomes

What are the main benefits for a student with dyslexia following DSA SMART START?

Students with dyslexia improve language comprehension and production through a visual and multisensory approach that makes learning more accessible and engaging.

How does DSA SMART START help overcome dyslexia-related difficulties when learning English?

The programme uses visual techniques, audio support and interactive activities to reduce reading and memorisation difficulties, helping students overcome their specific challenges.

What improvements in language comprehension and production can students with dyslexia expect?

Students can expect better listening and written comprehension, a richer vocabulary and greater confidence communicating in English, thanks to a structured approach supported by visual techniques.
